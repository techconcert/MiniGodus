What is new in MiniGodus r7:

- Height-aware walking. Nobody climbs a cliff any more: a step between two tiles is allowed only on the same layer, or between two neighbouring layers across a ramp. This holds for every walking path (villagers going to work, home, to drink, to bed and fidgeting; the shaman going to a project, to his bed, wandering). A ramp is a clearly drawn stair tile (vertical and horizontal variants, readable in every climate, under snow and at night) and the figure walks along it. Nobody is ever teleported or clipped through a wall: a villager whose ground is taken goes to the nearest tile that is connected to the green, which is very rare (see the test numbers).
- The shaman cuts ramps. A new project, CUTTING RAMPS, runs when a home, field or other building, the shore (while there is no well), a tree (when wood runs low) or the shaman himself cannot be reached over the ramps there are. Each ramp costs 2 faith and is cut where the planned route crosses between layers. Buildings are never laid out where the village could not walk to them, and a building is not put where it would cut the shore or the last trees off. A village or a loaded game gets its access ramps free of charge. Terraforming that takes a tile out of the ramp range (layer under 2 or over 6) or builds on it clears the ramp.
- Old saves. The ramp bits are saved in part 2 of the save (no version change). A save from r6 has no ramps: it loads, the shaman cuts access ramps where homes became unreachable, and nobody is stuck.
- Top bar. Two resources at a time, a big icon and the big number each (the r3 digit style): faith and population, then food and wood, alternating every 3.5 seconds. The clock is right aligned a few pixels left of the weather icon and the temperature, and four digits of faith and two of population fit without touching it.
- Switch Game Mode. The update page has a new box under Firmware update, "Switch game mode", with two buttons, GeekQuest and GeekQuestAI (the same box and script as in the GeekQuest firmware, without a MiniGodus button). Your browser (not the clock) reads that game's latest.json on its builds branch; it says so if that build is already running, otherwise it shows "<game> <version> (<commit>). Your saved game will not carry over." and a second button "Switch to <game> <version>". That button downloads the .bin, runs it through the same checks as a manual upload and then presses Upload and install. A failed fetch, download or check says so and installs nothing. Recovery change: the update page text and script only (GQ_RECOVERY_REV 13, no new route); the rescue hotspot and safe mode are untouched.
- Balance. Over 100 simulated worlds: see the PR for the table (median day 38, p10 35, p90 43). The counts of worlds that die out or stall are listed there too.

Screenshots: docs/screenshots (top bar pairs, villagers on a ramp, the shaman cutting a ramp, a terraced village).

- Walking notes. The test metric for "jumps" ignores a step aside of two tiles or less (the ground under somebody turned into a wall and he stands on the nearest tile that is connected to the green); a longer move counts, and is asserted at most 0.05 per world and day (0 long ones). When no tile is connected to the green at all, the shaman is put on his bed tile and a villager on the green: nobody is put on a tile that nobody can leave.

What is new in MiniGodus r6:

- The monument. When the town has 28 villagers (and it is at least day 21) the shaman raises a 3x3 monument in five visible stages, one every 2 days at the earliest, each paid with spare faith (1300, 2200, 3200, 4500, 6500). Its type comes from the world's seed: pyramid, stone circle, great tree, obelisk or temple, 25 stages of new art (monument.png, 192 cells, the same 16-colour world palette). The plot is marked out early (from day 5, while there is room) and the shaman saves his faith for the stages: only needs above 59 come before it. While he saves, the levelling of spare land pauses.
- The world ends. There is no end date: when the last stage stands, the next day is the worship day. Every villager walks to the monument at dawn and stays until dusk (20:00), bowing with a flame, and the band reads WORSHIP DAY.
- The summary. After the worship day the screen shows the world name, the days, the peak population, births, deaths and buildings built, with the monument. It stays until the clock next wakes up (your sleep schedule's wake time, the built-in 06:00 without a schedule; about 12 hours of uptime when the clock is not set); the sleep schedule still darkens the screen. Then a new world starts. When the village dies out, the summary comes within minutes and the next world starts the next morning.
- New worlds. A new seed is drawn from the clock's entropy; the last 4 worlds are remembered and a world never has the same landform AND climate as the one before. The Clock settings page (linked from the status page) has a Start a new world button with a confirmation; it starts the new world at once.
- Full save. The saved game is now 1688 bytes in 6 parts (version 5): every villager (48), every building, the whole map (heights, felled trees, stumps, saplings, ponds, snow tiles), the monument, the world statistics, the fish stocks, the animals and the seed history. It is one record over 6 slots of 320 bytes in 0x80000-0x82FFF; it is valid only when all 6 slots carry the same sequence number and record checksum, and a torn write falls back to the previous complete record. Saved every 20 minutes and at each new day. A version 4 save (r4, r5) loads and is upgraded by the next save.
- Balance. Over 100 simulated worlds no world ends before day 33: median day 37 (p10 35, p90 41, max 51), none dies out, the town has 20 villagers on day 15 (median) and the population reaches the 48 cap by day 30. The plateau of r5 is fixed: lumberjacks always find wood (brushwood from fallen branches when no tree is in reach, new saplings close to the green), a couple's children are balanced between the sexes, a young couple settles every 3 days while there is room and food, and a lone couple forages when the stores run dry.
- Animals and fish. Animals die (3% a day each, and one per pen and day in a lean spell) and a pen with a herder and enough food breeds again (a lost pair is replaced by a wild pair). Every water body has a fish stock (12 + 2 per tile, at most 250) that every catch depletes and that grows back by a tenth a day.
- Tools: tools/balance.cpp and tools/balance_run.py run the 100-world check (CI workflow balance.yml, about 3 minutes, in parallel with the build); debug commands GQ_DBG_MONUMENT (stage 0..5), GQ_DBG_PHASE (worship day, summary) and GQ_DBG_EXTINCT, also in the browser preview.
- Cost: static RAM +136 bytes in the game core (budget 1.2 KB) and none in the firmware (the 320-byte slot buffer is the only record buffer: the game is streamed in and out one slot at a time). Firmware size: see the build report.
- Tests: test/test_arc.cpp (monument, worship day, summary, extinction, 40 new worlds, animals and fish), test/test_reclog.cpp (the record log under random power cuts, every slot torn, the erase never touches the newest record, wear), test/test_store_sim.py (the real firmware/store.cpp on a simulated flash: 224 power cuts, an r5 record, the crash quarantine), test/test_save.cpp (version 5, version 4 fixture), test/test_version.py (VERSION matches this file).
- Safer settings page. The POST routes of the settings page (save, reset, night, retry, new world) refuse a request whose Origin or Referer names another site, so a web page elsewhere cannot start a new world or change the settings.
- Known limits of r6: the population cap is 48, so late-game towns plateau there; the worship day is one fixed day (dawn to 20:00); the summary ends after about 12 hours of uptime when the clock is not set; a new world from the web page loses the current one without a backup; the sleep schedule darkens the screen but the summary still waits for the wake time of the next day; the weather, the clouds and the villagers' walking state are not saved (they restart from the live reading and the roles); the monument stage costs are tuned for the typical town, a very poor world may need more than 50 days.

What is new in MiniGodus r5:

- Weather plays. The reading the clock already fetches (WMO code, temperature) now drives the game; with no reading the game is mild and dry. Rain and drizzle: animated rain, puddles on the low land, fields grow 50% faster, fishers catch more, people outside collect exposure. Thunderstorm: heavier rain, lightning, and everybody shelters at home (no outdoor work while it lasts). Snow: snow settles on the high layers in three steps and melts away again (the land is as it was), fields stop, firewood is needed. Above 28 C thirst comes twice as often and more water carriers are hired. Below 8 C each occupied home burns 1 wood a night (chimney smoke), lumberjacks come first, and a cold home with no wood raises sickness. Fog: haze. Clear and cloudy: drifting cloud shadows. The map is dimmed at night.
- Sickness. A daily risk that rises with hunger, thirst, cold and rain exposure. Sick villagers do not work, walk to the infirmary and show a green tunic and cross. An infirmary cures at the next midnight, no infirmary takes 3 days. Untreated, elders and weak villagers can die (25% a midnight); healthy fed adults never die of it. The shaman builds the infirmary when the first villager falls sick (hunger no longer calls for one) and a healer is hired when somebody is sick and there is an infirmary. Fix (pre-existing since r4): the shaman no longer retries an infirmary site he cannot reach for ever; tiles he cannot stand beside are skipped, failed sites rule out their neighbours, and a failed project puts its need on cooldown (regression test in test_weather).
- Wood. Lumberjacks fell trees (a stump stays), plant a sapling on the stump, and saplings grow into trees in 4 to 5 days (never on a building, a reserved plot, the green, or where it would shut a path). The forest seeds new saplings itself; the last 6 trees are left alone, so wood never runs out for good.
- Debug commands GQ_DBG_WEATHER, GQ_DBG_TEMP, GQ_DBG_SICK, GQ_DBG_AGE, GQ_DBG_FLASH and the query GQ_Q_TEMP10, for the tests and the screenshots.
- Cost: about 340 bytes of RAM (budget 1.0 KB); the saved game is still 192 bytes, version 4 (one more bit per villager), so an r4 save loads. Recovery layer, update page, hotspot, safe mode, the flash area 0x80000-0x82FFF and the size limits are unchanged.
- Tests: test/test_weather.cpp (WMO mapping and the no-reading default; rain boosts fields by 50%, snow and frost pause them; heat doubles thirst; cold burns one wood per occupied home per night; sickness rises with rain, thirst, hunger and cold homes; heals in 1 day with an infirmary, 3 without; kills untreated elders and weak villagers but not healthy fed adults after a rainy day; the shaman builds the infirmary; 20 days of felling keep the forest alive; snow settles and melts back to the same land; strips render; 20 days on 20 seeds with a mixed schedule of rain, storm, snow, heat and cold). test_shaman and test_village are unchanged. Saplings are turned into trees one per game minute, not all at midnight.
- Known limits of r5: the full multi-slot save is r6 (felled trees, stumps, saplings, snow and weather are not saved, so a restart brings back the forest as generated from the seed; sickness is saved for the first 16 villagers); no lit windows at night; the weather does not stop the shaman; rain and snow redraw the map 6 to 7 times a second.

What is new in MiniGodus r4:

- The shaman decides. Every 10 game minutes, when he is free, he scores the village's needs (food, housing, water, health, wood, growth, faith, monument, and a pond when there is no coast), adds a personality bias and starts the most pressing project he can afford. He carries it out one tile at a time, visibly: he walks beside the tile, raises his staff (new cast animation with sparkles), and the tile changes by one layer. The bottom band shows the project (DIGGING POND, FLATTENING PLOT, BUILDING HUT, CUTTING RAMPS). His robe colour shows his personality: farmer green, herder sand, fisher blue, builder red (picked from the world seed).
- Terraforming is paid in faith: a step between two layers costs the number of the higher layer (so it rises with height); clearing a tree or rock costs 1; rock (layer 7) is untouchable until the r6 monument. Faith is spent only when the cast happens and never goes below zero.
- Site search: for every building footprint he looks at the whole map and takes the spot of lowest cost = faith to level the plot (and the ramps it needs) + 2 per tile of walking distance from the village green + penalties for reserved tiles (a flat plot kept for the next home tier is a bonus for homes and a penalty for anything else). Buildings stand on one layer (3 to 5), never on the lowest land next to water, never on the keep-clear shore of a fishing hut, and never so that they cut the village in two or shut another building in.
- Rules: no coast within 12 tiles of the green means a pond of 6 water tiles (2x3 or 3x2) is dug next to the village and stocked with fish; when a home tier unlocks (6, 14, 24 villagers) a 3x3 plot beside the homes is flattened first; near everything he touches neighbouring tiles differ by at most one layer, he cuts ramps (a ramp sprite marks them) until no step is left; walking now needs steps of at most one layer, so a cliff blocks people until a ramp mends it. A tile that is pulled both ways by two fixed neighbours is left alone after one flip.
- All buildings, none bigger than 3x3: tent 1x1 (2 beds), hut 2x2 (4, from 6 villagers), cottage 3x2 or 2x3 (6, from 14), manor 3x3 (8, from 24), wheat field 2x2 to 3x3, orchard 3x2 or 2x3 (8 food per harvest, from 8), animal pen 3x2 to 3x3 (sheep, pigs or chickens; up to 10; they breed when a herder is there and there is food; from 10), fishing hut 2x1 or 1x2 on the shore, well 1x1, lumberjack hut 2x2 (from 4), granary 2x2 (raises the food cap by 100; from 10), shrine 2x2 (from 6; praying elders count 3 instead of 2 and it adds 1 faith per half hour), infirmary 2x2 or 3x2 (when somebody is weak; healing itself comes in r5). Bigger homes may replace tents and huts.
- Food variety: eating from 3 or more of the 4 sources (grain, fruit, animal, fish) in a day makes everybody content, +25% faith for the rest of that day. Food has a cap (8 per villager, at least 150, plus 100 per granary).
- Builders construct what the shaman lays out, in three visible stages. The herder role is now handed out once pens exist (r3 left it empty).
- Art: 15 new 16x16 cell groups for the buildings (up to 48x48 pixels), pen fences and animals, construction stages, orchard trees, ramps, and six shaman cast frames.
- Saved game format version 4: buildings are saved with their type and size (a code in 5 bits). The land is regenerated from the seed on load and healed at once (footprints levelled, ramps cut, free of charge); terraforming, ponds, plots and the shaman's project are not saved (the full save is r6). An r3 save is ignored and a new world starts. Recovery layer, update page, hotspot, safe mode, the flash area 0x80000-0x82FFF and the 500 KB limit are unchanged.
- Known limits of r4: the save keeps the first 16 villagers and 18 buildings only (finished ones first) and not the dug land; sickness and healing are r5 (the infirmary is built for the first weak villager); the monument is r6 (its need scores 0); animals do not die or get eaten; the fishing hut and the pond do not have a regrowing fish stock yet; fishermen always catch the same.
- Spare faith is spent: the faith cap is 9999 (the top bar shows four digits). When faith is above 40% of a reserve (60 + 4 per villager) and nothing urgent is open, he levels wide patches (4x4, 5x3, 3x5) near the green for the next tiers, preferring places next to forests and good soil, and digs a second pond for fish (band: LEVELLING LAND). Every cast is tested against the village staying in one piece, and trees that shut a building in are cleared. Rock (layer 7) now covers only the highest peaks, at most about 4% of the land (10% on the mountain plateau), so there is room to build. Contentment from food variety now counts on the day it is earned.
- Tests: test/test_shaman.cpp (20 days on 20 seeds under ASan: faith spent, terraforming, every building reachable, nothing over 3x3, nothing on the lowest shore land, faith never negative, population grows; every building type built in some seed; ponds on worlds without a coast; rock untouched; save round trip; at least 60% of the faith earned is spent and at least 36 casts per world; at most 2 bad slopes per world on average). test_worldgen checks the rock share. test_village keeps its checks; its limits changed for the new pace (see the PR).
